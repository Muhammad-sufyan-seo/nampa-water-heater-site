Bradford White Repair in Nampa Starts With the ICON Generation

Bradford White water heater repair in Nampa starts with the serial number. Bradford White's ICON System gas control comes in two generations, ICON 1.0 and ICON 2.0, and Bradford White documents a separate set of status light codes for each. Whether the unit sits in a Central Nampa utility closet in 83651 or a South Nampa garage in 83686, the generation decides how we read the light. Bradford White's service documentation splits the two generations by the serial date code:

  • ICON 1.0: serial date code before "TA" — units built before January 2019.
  • ICON 2.0: serial date code "TA" (January 2019) and later.

Bradford White's ICON 2.0 quick service guide applies to its atmospheric-vent and direct-vent gas water heaters with that serial date code. Read us the full serial number from the rating plate and we identify the generation before interpreting a single flash.

ICON 2.0 Status Light Codes

On ICON 2.0 gas controls, Bradford White's quick service guide defines the three status light patterns below, among others. Each pattern is a flash count followed by a pause before the sequence repeats.

Flash patternMeaningGeneration
1 flash, 4-second pauseNormal idle: control knob in pilot position, or thermostat satisfiedICON 2.0
4 flashes, 3-second pauseHigh tank temperature faultICON 2.0
10 flashes, 3-second pauseInsufficient combustion air detectedICON 2.0
Do Not Apply This Table to ICON 1.0 These three patterns are documented for ICON 2.0 controls with a serial date code of "TA" (January 2019) or later. An ICON 1.0 control reports through the code set in Bradford White's ICON 1.0 documentation, so we confirm the generation before acting on any blink pattern.

One Flash, Four-Second Pause: Not a Fault

On ICON 2.0, a single flash followed by a four-second pause means the control is idle: the knob is in the pilot position, or the thermostat is satisfied. If the water is cold while the light shows this pattern, check whether the control knob was left at the pilot setting before assuming a failed part.

Four Flashes: High Tank Temperature

Four flashes with a three-second pause means the ICON 2.0 control has detected a high tank temperature fault and shut down as a safety measure. That shutdown protects the household from overheated water, and a unit that repeats it needs its temperature sensing and gas control diagnosed.

Ten Flashes: Insufficient Combustion Air

Ten flashes with a three-second pause means the ICON 2.0 control has detected insufficient combustion air. That sends us to the air supply around and into the unit and to the venting before we look at any control part. Keep the area around the base of the water heater clear, and if a carbon monoxide alarm is sounding, leave the home and call 911. Combustion-side repairs fall under our Gas Water Heater Repair service, and a pilot that won't stay lit is covered in our Pilot Light Won't Stay Lit guide.

Other Bradford White Units We Repair

Not every Bradford White uses the gas ICON tables above. Two other groups follow their own documentation.

Electric Models With ICON Electronic Controls

Bradford White also uses the ICON System name on some residential electric water heaters with an electronic control. Those models have their own service manual and their own codes, and the gas ICON 1.0 and ICON 2.0 tables do not apply to them.

Standard Electric Tanks

A Bradford White electric tank without an electronic control is diagnosed by component: heating elements, the thermostats that switch them, and the high-limit switch. We meter each part before replacing it. Element and thermostat work is detailed on our Electric Water Heater Repair page.
