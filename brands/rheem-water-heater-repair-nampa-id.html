Rheem Repair in Nampa Starts With the Model Category

Rheem water heater repair in Nampa starts with one question: which Rheem is it? A Rheem gas tank in a Downtown Nampa basement, a Rheem tankless on a garage wall in South Nampa, and a Rheem hybrid heat pump in a newer 83686 home share a nameplate but not a diagnostic system. Rheem's gas tankless units report faults as numbered codes, Rheem's hybrid heat pump units display alarm codes such as A004 and A008, and a Rheem gas tank without a code display is diagnosed component by component. We identify the category first, then diagnose.

Rheem Error Codes by Model Category

Four Rheem codes appear below, each tied to the Rheem product family whose documentation defines it. A code from one family carries no meaning on another, so the "Applies to" column matters as much as the code itself.

Codes 11 and 12 on a Rheem Gas Tankless Unit

Code 11 means the burner never lit; code 12 means the burner lit and then lost its flame. Rheem's tankless documentation points service toward the same short list for both codes: the gas supply reaching the unit, the igniter and its electrode, the flame rod that proves flame to the control board, and the venting and combustion-air path. Carbon on the flame rod is one cause Rheem's own maintenance instructions address directly. A closed gas valve or an empty propane tank stops ignition just as surely as a failed part, so we confirm the gas supply before replacing anything. Tankless-specific service is covered in full on our Tankless Water Heater Repair page.

Alarms A004 and A008 on a Rheem Hybrid Heat Pump

A004 is a compressor shutdown alarm. Rheem's hybrid manuals describe it as a discharge–suction trip, after which the water heater falls back to its electric heating elements. Hot water keeps coming, but the unit is heating with its elements instead of its heat pump. Rheem's troubleshooting sequence for A004 starts by confirming the fan runs while the compressor is on.

A008 is a dry-fire alarm: the control has detected that the tank does not hold enough water. Rheem notes this is generally an installation issue caused by air left in the tank. The documented response is to fill the tank and purge the air by running a hot water faucet; if A008 returns, air is still present. Compressor, fan, and refrigerant-circuit work falls under our Heat Pump (Hybrid) Water Heater Repair service.

A Rheem Code Belongs to One Model Family Codes 11 and 12 come from Rheem's gas tankless documentation; A004 and A008 come from Rheem's hybrid heat pump manuals. Do not read either set onto a Rheem gas tank's status light or a standard Rheem electric tank, which follow their own model documentation. If your display shows a code not listed here, read it to us exactly as shown along with the model number, and we look it up against the manual for that model.

Rheem Gas Tank and Standard Electric Repairs

A Rheem tank-style water heater without a code display is diagnosed by component, not by code. The components differ by fuel type.

Rheem Gas Tank Units

On a Rheem gas tank that isn't heating, we check the pilot assembly and thermocouple, the gas control valve, the burner, and the venting and combustion-air path the unit's safety devices monitor. A pilot that lights and then goes out is covered step by step in our Pilot Light Won't Stay Lit guide; the repair itself falls under Gas Water Heater Repair.

Rheem Standard Electric Units

A dual-element Rheem electric tank heats with an upper and a lower element, each switched by its own thermostat, behind a high-limit switch that cuts power if the water overheats. We meter elements and thermostats rather than swapping parts by trial and error. Some newer Rheem electric models add an electronic control with its own codes, and those codes are defined in that model's manual. Element and thermostat repairs are detailed on our Electric Water Heater Repair page.

What to Note Before Calling About a Rheem

Five details let us arrive with the right diagnosis already narrowed down:

  • Photograph the rating plate so we have the exact model and serial number.
  • Copy the code exactly as displayed, letters and zeros included — A004, not "4."
  • Note when the code first appeared and whether it followed a drained tank, a gas shutoff, or a power outage.
  • Check whether other gas appliances in the home are working, which separates a gas supply problem from a water heater problem.
  • Record whether you still have any hot water, partial hot water, or none.

When a Rheem Repair Becomes a Replacement Question

Some Rheem faults end in a part; others end in a decision. A leak through the tank wall can't be fixed with a part, and a major component failure on an older unit raises the question of whether the money belongs in the old water heater or a new one. We lay out the repair and the alternative side by side, and our Repair or Replace Your Water Heater decision guide walks through the factors that matter.
