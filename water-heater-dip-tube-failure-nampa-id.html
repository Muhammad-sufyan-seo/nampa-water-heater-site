Water Heater Dip Tube Failure: One Possible Cause of Insufficient Hot Water

If your water heater isn't producing enough hot water, a failed dip tube is one of several things worth checking — not an automatic diagnosis. Here's what a dip tube does, where it fits among the other possible causes, and why checking it is a job for a technician.

What a Dip Tube Does

In a tank water heater, the dip tube is the pipe that carries incoming cold water down to the bottom of the tank, where it's heated, while hot water is drawn from the top. If the dip tube cracks, breaks apart, or isn't the correct length or spec for the unit, incoming cold water can mix directly with the hot water near the top of the tank instead of being routed to the bottom first.

Dip Tube Failure Is One of Several Possible Causes — Not a Diagnosis

Bradford White's own service documentation for its gas water heaters lists an out-of-spec dip tube diluting hot water with cold water as one of six possible causes under "Not enough hot water." It's listed alongside these other causes, with no priority order between them:

  • Thermostat/gas valve set too low for the desired water temperature
  • Thermostat/gas valve out of calibration, even if the dial looks correctly set
  • Cold inlet water temperature
  • High simultaneous hot water demand
  • A water heater that's undersized for the household
  • An out-of-spec dip tube diluting hot water with cold water

That matters for how this symptom gets diagnosed, and the reasoning runs in both directions. A correctly positioned thermostat dial doesn't prove the thermostat itself is calibrated — calibration is a separate issue from the dial setting. But even setting the thermostat aside entirely, that still wouldn't establish dip tube failure on its own, since cold inlet water, high demand, and an undersized unit can each produce the same symptom independently. A technician checks these systematically rather than assuming one cause once another is set aside. For a full walkthrough of diagnosing insufficient hot water, see our no hot water guide.

How a Dip Tube Problem Is Actually Confirmed

Checking a dip tube isn't a visual guess from outside the tank. Bradford White's service procedures for this component — documented separately for its Defender Safety System models and its PV Series through-the-wall models — call for shutting down the unit, draining the tank, and disconnecting the inlet water line so the inlet nipple and dip tube can be removed and inspected directly for cracks, blockage, or deformation. A dip tube that's cracked, restricted, or otherwise damaged is treated as not field-repairable and is replaced rather than patched.

That procedure is why this isn't a homeowner task: it involves draining a tank full of hot, pressurized water and disconnecting a plumbing connection, which both manufacturer manuals linked to this procedure restrict to qualified service providers. We handle the inspection and, if needed, the replacement as part of a full diagnosis — not as an isolated repair based on one symptom.
