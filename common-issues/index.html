What's Going Wrong With Your Water Heater?

Most water heater problems fall into one of six categories. Each one below links to a full diagnostic guide — likely causes for both gas and electric units, what's safe to check yourself, and when a symptom means stop and call a professional.

No Hot Water

Water runs cold or lukewarm. Usually a failed thermocouple or pilot issue on gas units, or a tripped high-limit switch or failed heating element on electric.

Diagnose No Hot Water

Water Heater Leaking

Water pooling at the base or dripping from a fitting. Could be a loose connection or valve — or tank corrosion, which means replacement, not repair.

Diagnose a Leak

Water Heater Making Noise

Popping, rumbling, or banging sounds are almost always sediment buildup on the tank bottom — common with Nampa's hard water.

Diagnose the Noise

Pilot Light Won't Stay Lit

Gas-unit-specific issue. Almost always a worn thermocouple — a quick, affordable fix once diagnosed correctly.

Diagnose the Pilot Light

Rusty or Discolored Water

Often a depleted anode rod allowing internal tank corrosion to begin — Nampa's hard water wears anode rods faster than average.

Diagnose Discolored Water

Breaker Keeps Tripping

Electric-unit-specific. Usually a failing heating element drawing excess current — never repeatedly reset it without a diagnosis.

Diagnose the Breaker Trip
When to Stop Troubleshooting and Call a Professional Some checks are safe to do yourself — checking a breaker, checking the gas supply valve is open, confirming the thermostat setting. But stop and call immediately if you smell gas near the unit, if a pilot won't relight after one attempt, if a breaker trips again after a single reset, or if you see water actively leaking from the tank itself. Guessing wrong on a gas or electrical issue is a safety risk, not just a repair cost.
