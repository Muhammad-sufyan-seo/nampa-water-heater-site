A burning or hot-electrical smell near your water heater is a different problem from a sulfur or rotten-egg odor — if that's what you're actually smelling, see our rotten egg smell from a water heater in Nampa, Idaho guide instead. Common Causes of a Burning or Electrical Smell

These causes run from the least urgent to the most urgent, since what the smell means — and what to do about it — depends heavily on which one applies.

  • New-Unit Break-In Odor — A brief, mild odor the first few times a brand-new heating element or gas burner runs is normal. It comes from manufacturing residue or dust on the new component burning off, and it resolves quickly as the unit completes its first few cycles.
  • Dust Burning Off a Dormant Burner — On a gas water heater that has sat unused for a while, such as a seasonal gap or an extended absence, dust that settled on the burner can produce a short burning smell the first time it fires again. This is similar in nature to the new-unit odor above and should clear within the first few minutes of operation.
  • Overheating Wiring or a Loose Connection — Damaged wire insulation or a loose, corroded electrical connection at the water heater can overheat and produce a burning or hot-electrical smell. This is a real fault, not a break-in odor, and it's the same underlying type of electrical problem that can cause an electric water heater's breaker to keep tripping — the smell and a tripping breaker are often two symptoms of the same wiring issue rather than two separate problems.
  • A Heating Element or Thermostat Beginning to Fail — A heating element or thermostat that is starting to fail can overheat before it fails completely, producing a burning smell at the unit. This is the most urgent cause on this list along with wiring faults, since the component is actively degrading.

Safe Homeowner Diagnostic Checks

These checks help you describe what's happening without opening the unit or touching any electrical component:

  1. Note when the smell started. A smell that appeared with a brand-new heating element or burner, or right after the unit sat unused for a while, points toward the break-in or dormancy causes above.
  2. Look, without touching, for scorch marks, discoloration, or melted insulation near the water heater's exterior or its electrical access panel.
  3. Feel the air near the unit for heat without touching any part of it — noticeably warm air near an electric water heater's access panel is a warning sign.
  4. Note whether the smell fades during normal operation or keeps returning. A smell that fades after the first few cycles is consistent with break-in or dust burn-off; one that returns or grows stronger is not.
  5. Check whether the breaker for the unit has also tripped around the same time as the smell, which points toward the wiring and connection causes above.

Never open the water heater's electrical access panel yourself — diagnosing and touching wiring, connections, or components is a job for a licensed technician.

When to Call a Professional Immediately

Stop using the water heater and call a professional immediately if any of the following apply:

  • The smell comes with heat, visible smoke, or scorch marks and discoloration near the unit
  • The smell persists beyond the first few uses of a brand-new heating element or burner
  • The smell fades and then returns, or grows noticeably stronger
  • The breaker for the unit trips at the same time as the smell
  • You're not sure whether what you're smelling is a burning or electrical odor versus a sulfur or gas smell

Turn off the breaker on an electric unit, or shut down a gas unit, and leave it off until a technician can inspect it.
