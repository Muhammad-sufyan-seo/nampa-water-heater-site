Flushing vs. Descaling: Two Different Services for Two Different Water Heaters

"Flushing" and "descaling" get used interchangeably, but they're not the same job. Flushing removes sediment from inside a tank water heater; descaling removes mineral scale from a tankless unit's heat exchanger. Here's what separates them, and how to tell which one your water heater actually needs.

Flushing and Descaling Are Not the Same Service

Flushing and descaling are two different maintenance actions, performed on two different types of water heater, to remove two different substances. Flushing drains sediment out of a tank water heater; descaling circulates a solution through a tankless water heater's heat exchanger to remove mineral scale. The confusion is understandable — both deal with mineral buildup caused by the same hard water passing through the unit — but which one applies depends entirely on whether the water heater stores water in a tank or heats it on demand.

What Flushing Removes, and From Which Water Heaters

Flushing applies to tank water heaters — gas, electric, and heat pump (hybrid) units that store a tank of heated water. The minerals in incoming water settle out and collect as sediment at the bottom of the tank, simply because the tank gives them a place to settle by gravity while the water above stays in use. Flushing is the process of draining the tank to clear that sediment layer out. Our water heater maintenance checklist covers tank flushing in full — why it's handled as part of a professional maintenance visit and what the visit covers alongside it.

What Descaling Removes, and From Which Water Heaters

Descaling applies to tankless water heaters, which heat water on demand as it passes through a heat exchanger instead of storing it in a tank. Because there's no standing tank of water for minerals to settle in, a tankless unit's mineral problem takes a different form: scale, a mineral coating that builds up directly on the heat exchanger's internal surfaces as water flows across them. There's no sediment layer to drain, so descaling doesn't involve draining the unit — it means circulating a descaling solution through the heat exchanger to dissolve that coating. Our tankless water heater repair page covers descaling in full, including the hard-water scale mechanism specific to tankless heat exchangers.

Why Asking for the Wrong One Doesn't Solve Anything

Requesting the wrong service isn't just a wording mix-up — it accomplishes nothing, because the problem that service is built to fix isn't present in that type of equipment. A tankless water heater has no tank and no sediment layer, so a flush has nothing to drain. A tank water heater doesn't run water through a heat exchanger the way a tankless unit does, so descaling isn't addressing the mineral problem that unit actually has, which is sediment at the bottom of the tank. Naming the right service starts with naming the right equipment: a tank unit (gas, electric, or heat pump) needs flushing for sediment; a tankless unit needs descaling for scale.

Both Are Professional-Only Maintenance

Flushing and descaling are both jobs we handle rather than homeowner tasks, and for related reasons tied to each procedure. Flushing means draining hot, pressurized water out of a tank and managing the unit's heat source while it's empty, as our maintenance checklist explains. Descaling means circulating a chemical solution through a tankless unit's sensitive internal components, as our tankless repair page explains. Either one done incorrectly risks the unit or whoever is standing nearby, which is why we don't publish do-it-yourself steps for either. For how often either service is due, follow the manufacturer's schedule for your unit rather than a single number that applies to every brand and model.

Which One Does Your Water Heater Need?

Start with the equipment, not the terminology. If you have a gas, electric, or heat pump water heater with a storage tank, the service it needs is flushing. If you have a tankless water heater — a wall-mounted unit with no tank — the service it needs is descaling. If you're not sure which type you have, or which service is overdue, our water heater maintenance service covers tank flushing and tankless descaling as separate services, matched to the equipment actually in front of us.
